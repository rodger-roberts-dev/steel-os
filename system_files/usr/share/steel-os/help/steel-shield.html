Steel Shield

Built-in, free virus protection. No subscription, no ads, nothing to sign up for.

What it does by itself

  • Downloads: every file that lands in Downloads is checked within seconds.
  • USB drives: when you plug one in, a notification asks Scan this drive? Programs and documents are checked first.
  • Weekly: your home folder is checked on Tuesday afternoon, at the lowest priority. It waits while you're playing a game.
  • Definitions: the list of known threats comes built in, so you are protected from the first start, even offline. Online it updates every hour, plus a daily list of brand-new threats from abuse.ch.

If it finds something

The file is moved to quarantine, where it can't run. Nothing is ever deleted without you choosing to. A notification says what it was, in plain English, with Restore and Details buttons.

Trust the file (for example a game mod or a tool you know)? Click Restore, or in Steel Shield choose Quarantine, the file, then Restore it and always allow this file.

Behaviour watching

Virus definitions only know threats that have already been seen. Steel Shield also watches what programs do, the way a Mac or Windows does:

  • Something sets itself to start when you log in. If it's an app from the App Store or part of the system, you get a quiet note. Anything else asks: Keep or Remove. Removed items are kept aside, not deleted.
  • A program changes lots of your files at once. If a program from Downloads, a temporary folder or a script suddenly rewrites or renames many files in Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Music or Videos, the way ransomware does, Steel Shield pauses it and asks: Stop it or Allow it.
  • A hidden program works the processor hard. A program from Downloads or a temporary folder using the processor heavily for minutes, the way hidden cryptocurrency miners do, is paused and you're asked the same. Games are never touched.
  • A downloaded file is made runnable. Steel Shield checks it first and reminds you it came from the internet.

Programs that come with Steel OS and apps from the App Store are never paused. Allow it remembers your choice. To switch behaviour watching off, open Steel Shield and click Turn off on the Behaviour watching card.

Block dangerous websites (optional)

In Steel Shield, Block dangerous websites for every app makes the whole computer look websites up through Quad9, a free non-profit service that refuses known malware and phishing sites. It asks for your password once. It's off until you turn it on.

Open Steel Shield

Start menu, Steel Shield, the shield on the taskbar, or the Steel OS Portal, Welcome, Steel Shield. The window has four sections on the left:

  • Status: the shield is blue when you are protected, and orange with a Fix now button when something needs attention. Below it, one card per protection (definitions, download scanning, behaviour watching, firewall and more), each with its own On/Off switch.
  • Scan: Quick scan (your files), Full scan (the whole computer: every user's files, settings, added programs and plugged-in drives; it can take an hour), Scan a folder or drive, or Scan a file. A progress bar shows how far it is, and you can keep working.
  • Quarantine: what was found, in plain English, with Restore and Delete.
  • History: every scan, fix and blocked file, newest first.

Fix now and the scans don't ask for a password. Restoring or deleting a file that the full scan found outside your own folders does.

Right-click any file or folder in Dolphin and choose Scan with Steel Shield.

Is this email a scam? (Check a message)

An optional add-on. On Steel Shield's Status page, click Add it on the Scam checker card. Check a message then appears on the left.

  1. Copy the whole email or message (from Gmail, Outlook, a text, WhatsApp, anywhere) and paste it in. If you have the email as a file, click Open an email file… instead.
  2. Click Check it. In a few seconds you get Looks safe, Be careful or Looks like a scam, with the reasons in plain English, for example a link says paypal.com but really goes to paypa1-secure.com, or sent as "Amazon" from a free gmail.com address.

It checks the sender, where every link really goes, attachments and the wording. Nothing you paste is sent anywhere: the check runs on your computer. When online it also asks Quad9 whether a link's website is a known scam site, sending only the site's name. A scam message can't talk its way past it: the verdict comes from fixed rules, not from an AI.

Report a scam so it gets blocked for everyone

When the verdict is Be careful or Looks like a scam, a Report this scam button appears under it. One click does two things:

  • Opens a message in your email app, addressed to the two groups that collect scams for the whole internet (APWG and Netcraft; their lists feed the browsers, mail providers and banks), with the message you checked in it. Press Send.
  • Opens Google's own report page in your browser with the scam link filled in. Press Submit Report. That gets the link blocked in Chrome, Firefox and Safari everywhere.

Nothing is sent until you press Send. Steel OS keeps no list of its own and never sees the message; the only thing it sends is a count, so the scams reported number on steel-os.org goes up by one.

The "Is this a scam?" button in Thunderbird

If you read email in Thunderbird (one click to install from the Portal, Steel Apps, Everyday), the same check can sit on every email:

  1. Open Thunderbird once and set up your email, if you haven't.
  2. In Steel Shield, Check a message, click Add the button to Thunderbird.
  3. Close Thunderbird completely and open it again. If it asks about a new add-on, click Enable.
  4. Open any email: Is this a scam? is in the message's toolbar. Click it for the verdict and reasons.
  5. If it is one, click Report this scam: Thunderbird opens a forward with the email attached, already addressed to APWG and Netcraft (press Send), and your browser opens Google's report page for the scam link (press Submit Report).

It checks the email right there on your computer; nothing from it is sent anywhere. To take the button out again: Thunderbird menu, Add-ons and Themes, Steel Shield: Is this a scam?, Remove.

No checker catches everything. When in doubt, don't click: open the company's website or app yourself and look there.

Report a problem or idea

At the bottom of Steel Shield's left-hand list, click Report a problem. It takes a screenshot of the screen as it was, then opens one window: choose Problem or Idea, give it a short title, say what happened (or what you would like), and click Send. You get a reference number back. Choose a picture… attaches a different screenshot or a phone photo instead. Untick Include the screenshot or Include these computer details if you would rather not send them. No account is needed, and nothing is sent until you click Send. It is the same report window as the Portal's Report a problem, so it lands with the same team.

The protection cards

  • Virus definitions: the list of known threats, and whether it stays current by itself (it checks every hour). Check now looks for new ones straight away.
  • Firewall: blocks connections to your computer that you didn't ask for. Turn off asks for your password; only do it to solve a specific problem, and turn it back on afterwards. Settings opens the full firewall app.
  • App sandbox: always on. It isn't a setting but how Steel OS is built: apps run in their own space, and the system files are read-only.

Turn on Secure Boot

Secure Boot checks that only trusted software starts your computer. It is a setting in the PC itself (the firmware), not in Steel OS, so it is switched on there:

  1. Restart, and straight away press the firmware key, usually Del, F2 or F10 (the start-up screen often says which).
  2. Find Secure Boot, usually under Boot or Security, and set it to Enabled. If it asks for a mode, choose Standard or Windows UEFI mode.
  3. Save and exit (often F10).

If Steel OS was installed with Secure Boot off, first follow Turning Secure Boot on after installing, or Steel OS won't start with Secure Boot on.

"Needs attention"

Steel Shield never shows a green tick when something is wrong. If the definitions are old or a part has stopped, it says so and offers Fix now, which downloads the newest definitions straight away and tells you whether it worked. A scan never says "no threats found" if it couldn't actually check.

Check that it works

Open Steel Shield, Scan, Test Steel Shield. It puts the harmless EICAR test file (which every antivirus treats as a virus) in your Downloads folder; within seconds Steel Shield should catch it and say how long it took. Then delete it from Quarantine. You can also download the same file from eicar.org (it may refuse connections from a VPN).

Steel Shield uses ClamAV with definitions from Cisco Talos. It is strongest against known threats; Steel OS's read-only system, signed updates and app sandboxes protect against the rest.
